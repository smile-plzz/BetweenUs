# Problem & Opportunity

## Problem statement

Couples generate large amounts of potentially useful shared context, but the software they use is optimized either for **communication now** or **organization later**. Very little software converts casual relationship context into useful future decisions without demanding maintenance.

### Fragmentation

Potential future experiences are scattered across:

- messaging threads;
- Instagram/Facebook/TikTok/Reels;
- YouTube;
- screenshots and camera rolls;
- browser tabs;
- maps;
- streaming watchlists;
- notes;
- calendars;
- shopping links;
- spoken conversations.

The problem is not merely storage. It is **loss of context**.

A URL saved without knowing who cared about it, why it mattered, whether the partner liked it, what it costs, where it is, or when it becomes relevant is only partially useful.

## Core pains

### P1 — Valuable ideas disappear in chronological communication

Messaging makes old information technically searchable but practically forgotten.

### P2 — Decision time requires re-searching

When the couple finally has free time, they often begin from zero: browse movies, search restaurants, ask each other what they want, become tired of choosing.

### P3 — Shared taste exists but is not computationally useful

Partners learn each other's preferences socially, but their software rarely represents the intersection well enough to reduce decisions.

### P4 — Organization itself is work

A conventional solution asks users to maintain lists, categories, tags and spreadsheets. That adds a second chore to the original activity.

### P5 — Digital presence is fragmented from utility

Couple apps often emphasize affection while productivity tools emphasize logistics. Real relationships contain both at once.

### P6 — Generic discovery creates abundance rather than confidence

Search engines and feeds can provide thousands of options. Couples frequently need three plausible options they are likely to enjoy.

## Opportunity

BetweenUs can occupy the layer between **conversation, memory and action**.

Instead of competing with Messenger or Instagram, it can receive durable fragments from them. Instead of competing with every specialized movie, recipe, travel or finance product, it can provide the couple-context layer that decides which specialized content matters.

## Why a two-person model matters

Most consumer software is designed around either:

- **one:** personal productivity, personal notes, personal recommendations; or
- **many:** social networks, groups, teams, communities.

A committed pair has different interaction dynamics. Decisions frequently require two preference signals, shared context accumulates over long periods, and emotional presence matters alongside utility.

The product hypothesis is that **exactly two** is not a limitation to work around; it is the design primitive.

## Primary opportunity statement

> When a couple encounters something they may want to remember or experience together, BetweenUs should make preserving it easier than losing it and make rediscovering it more useful than searching again.

## Secondary opportunities

Once the memory behavior exists, the same dataset can support:

- mutual recommendations;
- context-aware date decisions;
- gift recall;
- trip planning;
- lightweight shared goals;
- “what should we do?” decisions;
- playful presence;
- relationship history built from actual shared experiences rather than forced journaling.

## Key market risk

BetweenUs has a **two-sided adoption problem inside a single relationship**. One enthusiastic user cannot generate the full value proposition alone.

Therefore onboarding, invitation, immediate shared payoff and capture friction are more strategically important than feature count.

## Key product risk

The concept can expand almost indefinitely. Every shared-life category appears relevant. Without a disciplined wedge, BetweenUs could become a broad collection of mediocre features.

The solution is to preserve a broad vision while validating one repeated behavior first.
