# Behavior & Relationship Psychology

> Product framing, not clinical advice. BetweenUs should not diagnose, score or therapeutically evaluate relationships.

## Behavioral objective

The product needs a habit without becoming an engagement trap.

The desired habit is not “open BetweenUs every day.” It is:

> **When something feels relevant to us, save it to our shared space. When we need an idea, check our shared space before starting from zero.**

This is an event-driven habit rather than a daily streak.

## Behavioral loop

1. **Trigger:** encounter a movie, place, product, recipe, activity, thought or moment.
2. **Tiny action:** share/save to BetweenUs.
3. **Immediate reward:** partner can see/react; item is safely captured.
4. **Delayed reward:** the item resurfaces when useful.
5. **Accumulating value:** reactions and completed experiences improve future decisions.

The delayed reward is strategically important. It teaches users that saving something today produces value later.

## Attention as relationship currency

Many meaningful couple behaviors are small acts of attention: remembering a preference, noticing a mood, recalling an old suggestion. BetweenUs should preserve attention without quantifying it into a score.

Bad implementation:

- “You remembered 73% of your partner's wishes.”
- “Your partner sent 2× more gestures this week.”

Better implementation:

- quietly resurface the relevant thing when it can help someone act thoughtfully.

## Choice architecture

Couples often experience decision fatigue because each person is simultaneously searching, predicting the other's preference and negotiating constraints.

BetweenUs should reduce this through progressive narrowing:

**Shared history → current context → 3–5 plausible choices → lightweight mutual reaction → decision.**

The system should preserve the ability to browse more, but not make browsing the default path.

## Mutuality without forced sameness

A couple is not a single user. Two people can have different preferences.

The system should model:

- strong mutual likes;
- one-person enthusiasm + neutral partner;
- disagreement;
- unexplored possibilities;
- preference changes over time.

A “match” should not erase those differences. Explainable signals are preferable to a mysterious compatibility percentage.

## Presence

Presence can create closeness when it is intentional and expected. It can create pressure when it is ambient, irreversible or asymmetric.

Design rules:

- presence states should be understandable;
- sensitive presence should be opt-in and reversible;
- the same rules should apply to both partners;
- avoid “last seen” mechanics designed to trigger anxiety;
- avoid interpreting absence as relationship meaning;
- do not punish inactivity.

## Returning after absence

Do not exploit anxiety with unread counts, streak loss or “your partner is waiting” language.

The preferred mental model is **traces in a room**:

- a thing left for you;
- a few new shared possibilities;
- something timely from the archive.

The user should feel invited, not behind.

## Intimacy

The shared space may include sexual or romantic material between consenting adults. The psychology goal is not to manufacture intimacy; it is to reduce friction for expression that the couple already wants.

Important design implications:

- intimate actions should never be default-on;
- sensitive content needs discreet notification/preview controls;
- users should be able to change boundaries over time;
- avoid public/social mechanics, rankings or normative prompts about sexual frequency;
- never imply that declining/ignoring an intimate interaction is a negative relationship signal.

## Recommendation trust

A recommendation becomes more persuasive when the reason is legible.

Good:

> Both of you liked two similar comedies, and this one is already on your saved list.

Poor:

> 94% couple match.

The first supports agency. The second creates false authority.

## Engagement ethics

Success is not maximum screen time. A successful session may last 20 seconds and end with the couple putting their phones down to do something together.

Potential healthy success measures:

- captured ideas later resurfaced;
- resurfaced ideas acted on;
- mutual decisions completed;
- time from “what should we do?” to a decision;
- saved items becoming experiences;
- qualitative “put that in our app” behavior.

Avoid optimizing primarily for:

- daily active minutes;
- notification opens;
- streak length;
- volume of partner interactions.
