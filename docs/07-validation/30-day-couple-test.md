# 30-Day Couple Test

## Purpose

Test behavior before building the full digital-home vision.

## Core hypothesis

If capture is nearly effortless and saved ideas are usefully resurfaced, a couple will adopt BetweenUs as the durable destination for things they may want to experience together later.

## Participants

Start with the founding couple as a high-context pilot, but treat this as **founder testing, not market validation**. After usability stabilizes, recruit several external couples with different habits.

## Pre-test interview

Record baseline behavior:

- Where do you currently send movies/places/products?
- How often do you fail to find something later?
- How do you decide what to watch/eat/do?
- Do you maintain any shared lists?
- What makes a separate app feel worth opening?
- What kinds of information would feel uncomfortable in a shared couple app?

Do not teach participants the desired answers.

## Week 0 — Setup

- pair both users;
- demonstrate share-to-save once;
- add 2–3 real existing ideas;
- avoid preloading a fake large dataset;
- explain that normal messaging should continue normally.

## Week 1 — Capture test

Focus: **Will they save?**

Observe:
- captures per partner;
- share vs. manual entry;
- capture abandonment;
- categories naturally emerging;
- whether one partner does all the work;
- whether people remember the app at the moment of discovery.

Do not add new major features during the week unless a blocker makes capture unusable.

## Week 2 — Reaction + retrieval

Focus: **Does partner participation add value?**

Observe:
- reaction rate;
- time to partner reaction;
- searches/filters;
- whether users browse the collection without prompting;
- whether reactions feel useful or like homework.

## Week 3 — Decision test

Focus: **Can memory become action?**

At natural decision moments, use Watch/Eat/Do flows.

Observe:
- time to decision;
- whether suggested saved items are considered;
- whether the couple expands to generic search anyway;
- reasons candidates are rejected;
- whether explanations improve trust.

## Week 4 — Resurfacing + return

Focus: **Does accumulated history create value?**

Test:
- resurfacing an older forgotten item;
- “while you were away” composition;
- a simple contextual recommendation;
- completed-experience feedback.

## Quantitative indicators

Do not treat these as statistically validated thresholds in a tiny pilot. They are diagnostic.

### Capture health
- both partners contribute;
- capture continues after first-week novelty;
- median capture flow is very short;
- low failed-save rate.

### Shared engagement
- meaningful portion of items receive partner signal;
- neither partner is permanently passive.

### Memory activation
- old items are opened after resurfacing;
- saved items become real decisions/experiences.

### Decision utility
- decision mode reduces browsing/searching elsewhere;
- users accept at least some surfaced options.

## Qualitative success signals

Strong:
- “Put that in our app.”
- “Didn't I save that?”
- “Check what we had there.”
- user opens BetweenUs during a real decision without being asked;
- user is pleasantly surprised by something they forgot;
- users request a new category because they already trust the capture model.

Weak/negative:
- “I forgot the app existed.”
- “I'll just send it to you.”
- “Saving this is too much work.”
- one partner acts as librarian for both;
- collection grows but never affects decisions;
- users ask for chat because the core value is unclear.

## End-of-test interview

Ask separately before asking jointly:

1. What did you use BetweenUs for without thinking about it?
2. What felt like work?
3. Tell me about the last time it helped you decide something.
4. What did you still send only through chat, and why?
5. If the app disappeared tomorrow, what would you miss?
6. What would you *not* miss?
7. What information would you never want it to know?
8. What is the first capability you wish it had next?

## Decision framework after 30 days

### Evidence A: capture + decisions work
Proceed toward richer resurfacing, presence and discovery.

### Evidence B: capture works, decisions do not
Fix retrieval, metadata and timing. Do **not** expand scope.

### Evidence C: one partner uses it
Investigate pairing/onboarding/value asymmetry.

### Evidence D: capture does not stick
Revisit the wedge. Do not rescue the product by adding dozens of features.
