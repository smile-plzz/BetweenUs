# 30-Day Couple Test

## Purpose
Test behavior before building the full digital-home vision.

## Two hypotheses

### H1 — Memory habit
If capture is nearly effortless and saved ideas are usefully resurfaced, a couple will adopt BetweenUs as the durable destination for things they may want to experience together later.

Desired language: **“Put that in our app.”**

### H2 — Expression habit
If lightweight Question Cards make candid expression easier without becoming messaging or obligation, couples will voluntarily use them to create shared context.

Desired behavior: either partner naturally places a question/prompt in Our Space rather than needing the product to nag them.

Intimacy and Faith Together are important product dimensions, but the first pilot should validate their interaction primitives carefully rather than attempting to validate a comprehensive sexual or religious feature suite.

## Participants
Start with the founding couple as a high-context pilot, but treat this as **founder testing, not market validation**. After usability stabilizes, recruit external couples with different habits.

## Pre-test interview
Record baseline behavior around sharing ideas, losing them, decisions, shared lists, candid questions, app-install motivation, and sensitivity/privacy boundaries. Do not teach desired answers.

## Week 0 — Setup
- pair both users;
- demonstrate share-to-save once;
- add 2–3 real ideas;
- introduce one lightweight Question Card;
- avoid fake/preloaded history;
- explain normal messaging should continue normally;
- confirm shared-space visibility and discreet notification settings.

## Week 1 — Capture
Focus: **Will they save?**
Observe contribution balance, capture source, abandonment, emerging categories and whether the app is remembered at discovery time. Do not add major features unless capture is blocked.

## Week 2 — Reaction + expression
Focus: **Does partner participation add value?**
Observe reactions, retrieval, voluntary Question Card creation/response, Answer/Pass/Not-now behavior, whether cards feel candid/useful versus homework, and whether either partner feels pressure from unanswered cards.

Use primarily everyday/affectionate prompts. Carefully test a small optional set of adult-intimacy prompts only when both pilot participants intentionally enable that domain. Faith prompts/invitations should be tested as companionship, never worship completion tracking.

## Week 3 — Decisions
Focus: **Can memory become action?**
Use Watch/Eat/Do at natural moments. Observe time to decision, saved-item consideration, external-search fallback, rejection reasons and explanation trust.

## Week 4 — Resurfacing + return
Focus: **Does accumulated shared context create value?**
Test an older forgotten item, While-you-were-away composition, contextual recommendation, completed-experience feedback and a resolved/returned Question Card where appropriate.

## Diagnostic indicators
### Capture health
- both partners contribute;
- capture persists beyond novelty;
- short capture flow;
- low failed-save rate.

### Expression health
- both partners initiate at least some cards voluntarily;
- responses include honest disagreement/pass/not-now without product friction;
- unanswered cards do not create guilt;
- users distinguish cards from ordinary chat;
- intimate/faith categories feel contextually appropriate rather than tonally flattened.

### Memory activation
- old items reopen after resurfacing;
- saved items become real decisions/experiences.

### Decision utility
- decision mode reduces browsing elsewhere;
- users accept some surfaced options.

## Strong qualitative signals
- “Put that in our app.”
- “Didn't I save that?”
- “Check what we had there.”
- opening BetweenUs during a real decision without prompting;
- placing a Question Card without being prompted by the test;
- pleasantly rediscovering something forgotten;
- requesting a domain because the shared primitives already feel useful.

## Negative signals
- “I forgot the app existed.”
- “I'll just send it to you.”
- “Saving this is too much work.”
- one partner becomes librarian;
- collection never affects decisions;
- Question Cards feel like another inbox;
- sexual prompts feel coercive/clinical/gimmicky;
- faith features feel like piety monitoring;
- users ask for full chat because the differentiated value is unclear.

## End-of-test interview
Interview separately before jointly. Ask what became automatic, what felt like work, last useful decision, what remained in chat, what would be missed if removed, what data they would never want stored, whether Questions changed communication, whether Pass/Not-now felt safe, and what capability they want next.

## Decision framework
### A — Capture + decisions + expression show pull
Proceed toward richer resurfacing/presence and deepen the strongest relationship dimension.

### B — Capture works; decisions weak
Fix retrieval, metadata and timing. Do not expand scope.

### C — Capture works; Questions weak
Keep memory wedge; redesign or deprioritize expression rather than forcing engagement.

### D — Questions work; capture weak
Investigate whether expression is actually the stronger wedge before expanding the product.

### E — One partner uses it
Investigate onboarding/value asymmetry and pressure dynamics.

### F — Neither loop sticks
Revisit the product wedge. Do not rescue it by adding dozens of features.
